## Cultural notes

Besides the lessons in pronunciation and grammar, all of the dialogs key on some cultural issue that Valdo and Michelle have noticed as Brazilians living in the United States.

Brazilian gas stations always have attendants to help customers to pump their gas, change their oil, and wash their windshields, etc., unlike gas stations in the U.S. where the drivers do it all themselves.

## Grammar notes

If there were two Brazils, in Portuguese that would be <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>Brasis</mark></i>! How weird is that? The plural forms of words that end in "l" may look strange to speakers of English and Spanish, but the rules are actually fairly easy, basically drop the "l" and add "is":

- AL: <i lang="pt-BR">animal</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>animais</mark></i>; <i lang="pt-BR">radical</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>radicais</mark></i>; <i lang="pt-BR">nacional</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>nacionais</mark></i>
- EL: <i lang="pt-BR">papel</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>papéis</mark></i>; <i lang="pt-BR">túnel</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>túneis</mark></i>
- OL: <i lang="pt-BR">espanhol</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>espanhóis</mark></i>; <i lang="pt-BR">farol</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>faróis</mark></i>; <i lang="pt-BR">sol</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>sóis</mark></i>
- UL: <i lang="pt-BR">azul</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>azuis</mark></i>

This basic rule does have a few twists and turns. The first is when a word ends in "il", there would be no reason to have double "ii":

- IL: <i lang="pt-BR">barril</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>barris</mark></i>; <i lang="pt-BR">infantil</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>infantis</mark></i>

Another exception is if the word ending in "il" is not stressed on the last syllable. In those cases, we change things to "eis". This is one of those rules that we accept without worrying about why it is what it is:

- <i lang="pt-BR">difícil</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>difíceis</mark></i>; <i lang="pt-BR">fácil</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>fáceis</mark></i>; <i lang="pt-BR">fóssil</i> → <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>fósseis</mark></i>

Finally, there are a few words that just want to do their own thing, side-stepping the normal tendencies all together:

- <i lang="pt-BR">mal</i> → <i lang="pt-BR">males</i> (evils); <i lang="pt-BR">gol</i> → <i lang="pt-BR">gols</i>
