## Cultural notes

Besides the lessons in pronunciation, all of the dialogs key on some cultural issue that Valdo and Michelle have noticed as Brazilians living in the United States.

If you are a vegetarian, this cultural note isn't all that exciting. If, however, you are a meat lover, you are in for a treat. In Brazil, <i lang="pt-BR">churrascarias</i> are generally all-you-can-eat restaurants with a system called <i lang="pt-BR">rodízio</i> where you pay a pre-set price. Most even have a red light/green light system. If you put up the green light, the waiters keep coming around to bring you more cuts of meat, sometimes 25 – 30 different cuts. No wonder Michelle and Valdo call US Steak Houses a "snack house". On a recent visit to a churrascaria in Rio, these are the different cuts that were brought to Orlando's table: <i lang="pt-BR">costela de porco, frango, lingüiça, picanha, filé mignon com queijo, alcatra, filé mignon no sal, lombo de porco, fraldinha, paleta de carneiro, coração de galinha, filé mignon com bacon, costela de boi, galeto no sal grego, lingüiça calabresa pimentada, salmão grelhado, contra filé, lombinho com queijo, costelinha de carneiro, picanha fazendeiro, ponto de picanha, cupim</i>. We aren't even going to try and translate them, but we encourage you to go try them.

## Pronunciation notes

This is really one of the easiest pronunciation lessons. The rule is simple, direct, and without exceptions. In Portuguese "ch" always sounds like the English "sh". Try saying all of these words with an "sh" sound: <i lang="pt-BR">ma<mark>ch</mark>o</i>, <i lang="pt-BR">a<mark>ch</mark>ar</i>, <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>ch</mark>urrasco</i>, <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>ch</mark>egar</i>, <mark>Ch</mark>icago (well that one was easy!).

One more hint for Spanish speakers, not always, but many words that are spelled with "ll" in Spanish, now are spelled with "ch" in Portuguese: <i lang="es">llamar</i> (<i lang="pt-BR"><mark>ch</mark>amar</i>), <i lang="es">llegar</i> (<i lang="pt-BR"><mark>ch</mark>egar</i>) <i lang="es">lleno</i> (<i lang="pt-BR"><mark>ch</mark>eio</i>), <i lang="es">lluvia</i> (<i lang="pt-BR"><mark>ch</mark>uva</i>).
