## Cultural notes

Besides the lessons in pronunciation, all of the dialogs key on some cultural issue that Valdo and Michelle have noticed as Brazilians living in the United States.

Brazilians seem to always notice how hard Americans close their car doors. The phrase "<i lang="pt-BR">Você não tem geladeira em casa?</i>" (Don't you have a fridge at home?) may sound strange, but it is a way of saying, "Don't slam my car door!". We're sure that more than once, Brazilians have thought that the Americans were somehow angry at something, because of the way that they slammed a door when getting out of a car.

## Pronunciation notes

This dialog focuses on the sound <span lang="pt-BR-fonipa">/u/</span>. This sound is similar to what is heard in English in words such as *wh<mark>o</mark>'d*, *h<mark>oo</mark>t*, *b<mark>oo</mark>t*.

Excluding diphthongs and nasalization, this sound is found in two main locations:

- Any word written with the vowel "u": <i lang="pt-BR">p<mark>u</mark>xa</i>, <i lang="pt-BR">ed<mark>u</mark>cado</i>, <i lang="pt-BR">t<mark>u</mark>do</i>
- Any word written with a final "o": <i lang="pt-BR">esper<mark>o</mark></i>, <i lang="pt-BR">chat<mark>o</mark></i>, <i lang="pt-BR">certinh<mark>o</mark></i>

The challenge for learners who speak English is to avoid pronouncing "u" as one would in the words *h<mark>oo</mark>d*, *p<mark>u</mark>t*. Another challenge is to avoid pronouncing "u" as one would in the words *<mark>u</mark>niversity*, *<mark>u</mark>sual*. Compare for example the English *<mark>u</mark>niverse* and *p<mark>u</mark>dding* with the Portuguese <i lang="pt-BR"><mark>u</mark>nivers<mark>o</mark></i> and <i lang="pt-BR">p<mark>u</mark>dim</i>.

The challenge for learners who speak Spanish is to avoid pronouncing the word-final "o" as one would in Spanish <i lang="es">beb<mark>o</mark></i>, <i lang="es">dig<mark>o</mark></i>, <i lang="es">habl<mark>o</mark></i>. Compare for example the Spanish <i lang="es">com<mark>o</mark></i> with the Portuguese <i lang="pt-BR">com<mark>o</mark></i>.
