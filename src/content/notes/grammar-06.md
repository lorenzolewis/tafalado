## Cultural notes

Besides the lessons in pronunciation and grammar, all of the dialogs key on some cultural issue that Valdo and Michelle have noticed as Brazilians living in the United States.

In Brazil people go to cafes to socialize with friends, but they don't normally go to cafes to study. The idea of opening up your books and studying in cafes seems a little strange. Brazilians prefer to study at home or at a library.

## Grammar notes

If there is one verb that we wish we could transfer to Spanish it would be <i lang="pt-BR">ficar</i>. There is a wide range of uses for <i lang="pt-BR">ficar</i> in Portuguese that just leaves you wondering what to use in Spanish. Here are a few of those uses:

- To stay, remain: <i lang="pt-BR">Eu <mark>fiquei</mark> no Brasil por 8 meses.</i> (I stayed in Brazil for 8 months.)
- To be located: <i lang="pt-BR">O banco <mark>fica</mark> na esquina.</i> (The bank is located on the corner.)
- To become, get: <i lang="pt-BR">Marcos <mark>ficou</mark> doente.</i> (Marcos got sick.)
- To keep on, continue: <i lang="pt-BR">Alex <mark>fica falando</mark> e não diz nada.</i> (Alex keeps on talking, but doesn't say anything.)

Note that Spanish has different ways of dealing with each of the instances where <i lang="pt-BR">ficar</i> is used in Portuguese.

- To stay, remain: <i lang="es">Me quedé en Brasil.</i> (<i lang="es">Quedar</i> is often used for the meaning of stay or remain.)
- To be located: <i lang="es">El banco está en la esquina.</i> (The verbs <i lang="es">ser</i> and <i lang="es">estar</i> are often given as <i lang="pt-BR">ficar</i> in Portuguese.)
- To become, get: <i lang="es">Marcos se puso enfermo</i> or <i lang="es">se enfermó</i>. (Spanish uses reflexive pronouns to give the idea of becoming, something that is not common in Portuguese.)
- To keep on, continue: <i lang="es">Alex continúa hablando y no dice nada.</i> (The ongoing sense to keep on is better rendered with <i lang="es">continuar</i> or <i lang="es">seguir</i> in Spanish.)

Welcome to the verb <i lang="pt-BR">ficar</i>! Once you start using it, you'll wish you could add it to your Spanish collection too.
