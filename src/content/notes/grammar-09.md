## Cultural notes

Besides the lessons in pronunciation and grammar, all of the dialogs key on some cultural issue that Valdo and Michelle have noticed as Brazilians living in the United States.

For a Brazilian, the way some Americans dress would sometimes be a little cheesy, like wearing jeans with flip-flops, shiny dresses, a lot of make up and extravagant hairdos during the day time etc.

## Grammar notes

Possessive pronouns are those that mean *my*, *your*, *his*, *her*, *our*, and *their*. In Spanish things are fairly direct: <i lang="es">mi</i>, <i lang="es">tu</i>, <i lang="es">su</i>, <i lang="es">nuestro</i>, etc. Things are a little more complicated in Portuguese. Look at the following charts to summarize things.

### Chart #1: my

| Form | Portuguese | English | Spanish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masc & Sing | <mark>meu livro</mark> | my book | mi libro |
| Fem & Sing | <mark>minha casa</mark> | my house | mi casa |
| Masc & Plural | <mark>meus livros</mark> | my books | mis libros |
| Fem & Plural | <mark>minhas casas</mark> | my houses | mis casas |

### Chart #2: your

| Form | Portuguese | English | Spanish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masc & Sing | <mark>seu livro</mark> | your book | tu libro |
| Fem & Sing | <mark>sua casa</mark> | your house | tu casa |
| Masc & Plural | <mark>seus livros</mark> | your books | tus libros |
| Fem & Plural | <mark>suas casas</mark> | your houses | tus casas |

Note: Some speakers use <i lang="pt-BR">teu</i> and <i lang="pt-BR">tua</i>, but generally <i lang="pt-BR">seu</i> and <i lang="pt-BR">sua</i> are used.

### Chart #3: his

| Form | Portuguese | English | Spanish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masc & Sing | <mark>o livro dele</mark> | his book | su libro |
| Fem & Sing | <mark>a casa dele</mark> | his house | su casa |
| Masc & Plural | <mark>os livros dele</mark> | his books | sus libros |
| Fem & Plural | <mark>as casas dele</mark> | his houses | sus casas |

Note: Some speakers could use <i lang="pt-BR">seu</i> and <i lang="pt-BR">sua</i> but most Brazilians eliminate ambiguity with <i lang="pt-BR">dele</i>.

### Chart #4: her

| Form | Portuguese | English | Spanish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masc & Sing | <mark>o livro dela</mark> | her book | su libro |
| Fem & Sing | <mark>a casa dela</mark> | her house | su casa |
| Masc & Plural | <mark>os livros dela</mark> | her books | sus libros |
| Fem & Plural | <mark>as casas dela</mark> | her houses | sus casas |

Note: Some speakers could use <i lang="pt-BR">sua</i> and <i lang="pt-BR">suas</i> but most Brazilians eliminate ambiguity with <i lang="pt-BR">dela</i>.

### Chart #5: our

| Form | Portuguese | English | Spanish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masc & Sing | <mark>nosso livro</mark> | our book | nuestro libro |
| Fem & Sing | <mark>nossa casa</mark> | our house | nuestra casa |
| Masc & Plural | <mark>nossos livros</mark> | our books | nuestros libros |
| Fem & Plural | <mark>nossas casas</mark> | our houses | nuestras casas |

Note: Speakers could also use <i lang="pt-BR">da gente</i>, as in <i lang="pt-BR">o livro da gente</i>.

### Chart #6: their

| Form | Portuguese | English | Spanish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masc & Sing | <mark>o livro deles</mark> | their book | su libro |
| Fem & Sing | <mark>a casa deles</mark> | their house | su casa |
| Masc & Plural | <mark>os livros deles</mark> | their books | sus libros |
| Fem & Plural | <mark>as casas deles</mark> | their houses | sus casas |

Note: Brazilians will also say <i lang="pt-BR">o livro delas</i>, <i lang="pt-BR">a casa delas</i>, <i lang="pt-BR">os livros delas</i>, <i lang="pt-BR">as casas delas</i>, if we are talking about females who possess things.

Things are more complicated in Portuguese because Brazilians try to avoid ambiguity that is allowed in Spanish. In Spanish a phrase like <i lang="es">su casa</i> could mean your, his, her, or their house. Context usually takes care of things. In Portuguese the ambiguity is directly resolved with different forms.
